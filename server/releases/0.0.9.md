## What changed since 0.0.8

- The QR scanner reads the box's code at distances and angles where it did not. Until now a
  dense code (the enrolment code is 57 to 61 modules a side) read only in a narrow band: close
  enough to resolve, not so close that it filled the frame, and held square. Four causes, all in
  the app's own reader, found by drawing a real code into camera-sized frames and counting what
  read:
  - The scan for the three corner squares tried only every third dark run on a line as a
    square's beginning. On a code shown on a dark page, the corner beside the page was never
    found by any line through it. "Two corners of three" was the scanner's commonest near-miss.
  - A dense code's own data contains dozens of places that look like a corner along one row
    and one column, and three of those often made a neater right angle than the real corners.
    A real corner reads as one along its diagonals too, so those are tried first.
  - A turned code's module size was measured on a slant, which put its size out by a version
    or more. A code at forty-five degrees never read.
  - A speck of data could be taken for the small square near the fourth corner, and the grid
    was then sampled askew. The grid is also sampled from the three corners alone, and the
    decoder judges.

  Out of fourteen frames each, before and after: at 8 pixels a module 3 and 14; at 10 pixels 0
  and 13; at 3 pixels turned twenty degrees 1 and 12; at 6 pixels turned forty-five degrees 0
  and 13; at 3 pixels, slightly blurred, 2 and 14. Pointing at nothing costs under a third of
  the work it did, which is what warmed the phone during a long scan.
- The scanner's hints go when the code does. "Move closer" used to stay on the screen after the
  code had left the frame, and was still there the next time the scanner opened. It now also
  shows for the case that needs it most, a code so far away that only two corners resolve, and
  "move back" starts where a code of that size stops fitting the frame, not before.
- "Camera busy, tap to retry" retries on a tap (it did nothing). If the camera was refused, the
  scanner offers to ask again, and once Android has stopped asking, opens the app's page in
  the phone's settings; the scanner starts by itself when you come back.
- The map moves more smoothly. Dragging or pinching used to rebuild the whole screen around
  the map on every frame, restart three background tasks and work out the wanted map tiles
  again, all to redraw one layer. The camera is now watched from outside the screen's layout:
  a moving map redraws and nothing else happens, and the tiles under the view are worked out
  again only when its edge crosses into a new tile.
- GALLERY lists every photo of a burst. The list is read a page at a time, each page starting
  after the last photo of the one before, and "after" was by the time alone: when a page ended
  inside several photos taken in the same second, the rest of that second was never listed.
  The same for a day's photos on its page. The box now pages by time and hash.
- Three lists could stop the app on a repeated row: a strip of photos holding the same photo
  twice, two coins whose symbol and rank run together to the same text, and a gallery page
  from an older box handing a photo back. Each row's key is unique now.

In the repository and not in the release:

- `app/ios` is the beginning of an iOS app, from a contributor: one screen that says to connect
  the app to a box, and nothing behind it yet (no pairing, no channel to the box). The Android
  app is still the only client, and a release carries no iOS app.
- The engineering journal (`server/tools/DEV_UPDATE.md`) keeps the current month whole and
  each month before it as a line a day. The entries as they were written are in its history,
  and the tag v0.0.8 holds all of them to 13 November.

## Known gaps at 0.0.9

- Everything under [0.0.8's known gaps](0.0.8.md#known-gaps-at-008) still stands; nothing on
  that list is closed yet.
- The scanner still does not read a code turned forty-five degrees at about four pixels a
  module, or one seen at a steep slant (the far side a quarter narrower than the near), or one
  blurred across two pixels at that size. Square-on or straighter, closer or steadier, it does.
- The scanner's changes were measured on drawn frames, not on a phone's camera; the map's on
  how often the screen is rebuilt, not on a frame counter. Both want a look on a real device.
