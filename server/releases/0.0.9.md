# LocalGhost wisp 0.0.9

In progress, since 13 November 2026. The ninth cut of wisp; the name is the release line, the
number the cut. Everything in [0.0.1's notes](0.0.1.md) still holds, and the pages for
[0.0.2](0.0.2.md), [0.0.3](0.0.3.md), [0.0.4](0.0.4.md), [0.0.5](0.0.5.md), [0.0.6](0.0.6.md),
[0.0.7](0.0.7.md) and [0.0.8](0.0.8.md) say what each cut changed.

At the cut this page gets its date line, CITATION.cff its version and date, RELEASES.md its
row, and the README its status paragraph and line counts.

## What changed since 0.0.8

- Asking aloud works the way a microphone should. The dot beside the field is a microphone
  now; one tap and it listens, a bar shows how loud it hears you, and it stops by itself about
  two seconds after you stop (a second tap stops it at once). While the box works out the
  words there is a clock and a way to drop it, and the line says how long it took. Under that:
  - **What is sent is what you said.** The room's hum at both ends is cut off before it goes,
    and nothing goes when nobody spoke. A speech model handed silence writes words of its own
    ("Thank you."); the box now also refuses to hand it a file with no sound in it.
  - **A question goes first.** It used to wait behind any voice note being transcribed, minutes
    for a long one, and ran as background work. The note is now set aside and finished
    afterwards, and the question gets more of the processor.
  - **The right language.** A few seconds of speech are too little to tell a language by. The
    box is told the languages your phone is set to and knows the ones your voice notes were
    in; a short question heard as some other language is heard again as the one you speak most.
  - **Three faults gone.** A quick double tap left "listening" on the screen for good. Leaving
    the chat while the box was still working left your question in the recorder, where the
    check-in offered it as a voice note and a restart filed it in the journal, though a
    question is meant to be heard and forgotten. And tapping the chat's microphone deleted a
    check-in voice note you had recorded and not yet saved.

- What this cut added is in the status views, not only in its own pages. Box Status had no
  page for ghost.voiced at all: it now shows the questions asked aloud (how many, how long
  the last took, the average), a voice note set aside for one, and the notes, their hours
  and their languages. ghost.oracled's page shows the arbiter's own model, or that the box
  has none and which model reads cold. ghost.shadowd's says which model reads cold. The GPU's
  page lists the arbiter's model under what runs on the CPU by choice. `tools/health.sh`
  prints the same three things, and the Speech card under INTEGRATIONS the last question's time.

- The box checks itself, afterwards. ghost.shadowd was a daemon with one detector that never
  spoke (below). It now does the three things the Hard Truths posts ask of it, all after an
  answer is saved and none of them in the way of one:
  - **It reads its own answers cold.** When you ask the box something that is a matter of
    judgement ("should I", "was I wrong", "what do you think"), ten minutes after it answered it
    asks the same question again with no memories, no history and nothing about you, and
    compares the two. If they part, it tells you what the cold read said, and that the cold
    read is not a ruling. A model that knows you agrees with you more; this is the answer with
    you taken out.
  - **It reads its own conduct.** A chat is looked at for the box changing its answer when you
    pushed back with no new fact, pressing on after you gave a reason of your own, and asking a
    run of questions that only fit the answer it then recommends. Each finding quotes the box's
    own sentence, and one whose quote is not in the chat word for word is dropped.
  - **It keeps an audit trail.** For any chat: what the box asked you, in order, what it had
    been given when it wrote each answer, and its reasoning as it was saved.
  - **It counts what the box already holds.** Long sittings and ones after midnight. Your own
    sentences that give what is already spent as the reason to go on, or a fixed description of
    yourself as the reason. How many of your chats are about how you feel. Mail that leans on
    urgency, scarcity, social proof, authority, a gift or a commitment already begun, marked on
    the mail with the words it used. How old the memories the box brings to your chats are. How
    often the box sides with you, and how often it opens with praise.
  - **You can ask for it now, and it says so where you are.** The cold read of a chat you are
    in can be asked for at once instead of ten minutes on. And what it names about a chat is
    said in that chat as well as in a notification: the next time you write there, the first
    grey line above the answer says what was named about the answer before.
  - **It can have a model of its own.** A second opinion from the same model is the same
    opinion with less to go on. Put a second model, from another lab, on the box
    (`ai-models/arbiter-<name>.gguf`) and the cold read is asked of that one. It runs on the
    CPU beside the main model, in its own queue, so it never holds up a photo being described
    or a chat. A box without one reads with its main model and says so on every record.
  - **It reads what you wrote about other people, and keeps that to itself.** Three detectors
    read your own notes, check-ins and messages for sentences where you write that someone told
    you it did not happen, that raising something ended with you at fault, or that you need
    someone's leave. Three in a month are put side by side for you to find. They are never sent
    to the phone as a notification: a lock screen is no place for them.
  - **SHADOW, in the app (Android).** Every detector with how often it has spoken, its
    threshold and mute, what it has named, the thirty patterns with what is built and why the
    rest is not, and for any chat its audit trail and a button to have it read cold now. A mail
    that leans on pressure says so on its own page.
  - **You can tune it and you cannot turn it off.** Each detector has a threshold that moves
    inside published bounds, and can be muted for three days at most; while muted it goes on
    recording. Every change is logged, three changes in a row toward quiet are themselves named,
    and every threshold goes back to its baseline every ninety days.

  It names what it sees and stops: nothing is blocked or refused. Seventeen detectors run, over
  fourteen of the thirty patterns in its catalogue (the twenty-eight of "A ghost should not
  possess" and two from the posts before it); the other nineteen are listed as not built, each
  with its reason. `docs/SHADOW.md` has all of it.
- The one detector the box had never fired. It compared this week's messages with last week's,
  and it compared the chat's times (milliseconds) with seconds, so last week was always empty.
  Box Status showed "your messages (7d)" as every message ever sent for the same reason. Both
  count weeks now.
- The chat asks how much answer you want: a third switch at the top, beside incognito and web,
  goes short, medium, long. Short is the answer in one to three sentences. Medium is the chat
  as it was. Long is the story: the answer first, then the background, the history, the details
  and what your own memories add, with the web findings cited by number. For a long answer the
  phone searches even when auto would not have (as long as the box wrote searches for the
  question), runs all of them, and reads up to six pages where it read three; the box brings
  five of your memories where it brought two, and its Wikipedia article's sections after the
  opening where it gave one at most; and the answer gets room to finish after the model thinks.
  Web set to off stays off, the trail says so, and the long answer is written from the box's own
  sources. A search the box held back because it carried your own details is not sent, and what
  the box keeps itself (prices, the news) is not searched for, whatever the length. The phone's
  own model, when there is no box, takes the switch too.
- The box's Wikipedia section reaches the model whole. The section a question points at was
  added after the article's opening and then cut with it at the opening's length, so after a long
  opening only a few of its words were left.
- A weekend away is a trip. A trip needed 24 hours between its first photo and its last, so
  two days that began with Saturday's lunch and ended with Sunday's were listed as two days
  out, and since 0.0.8 they were folded into their month. A trip is now a night away: the clock
  where the photos were taken passed four in the morning, with eight hours or more between the
  first photo and the last. A day out, however long, is still an outing.
- A trip inside the country you live in is named by its places ("Bath and Bristol, 17 to 18
  October 2026"), not by the country. The box takes that country from the photos taken at home.
- Two places on one day are two memories. Photos more than 30 km apart begin a new outing, and
  two outings that began on the same day were written to one memory, the later over the earlier,
  at every pass. The morning's place was lost, and the trip it belonged to looked shorter than
  it was. Each has its own now.
- A stop with one or two photos is kept. It was no memory by itself and was dropped, so dinner
  on the evening of arriving, in a different town from the next day's, took the first day of
  the trip with it. It joins the outing beside it.
- Nothing to run after the upgrade. The box groups its photos and chains its trips again at
  its first pass, some ten minutes after the unlock, and a trip that had been folded into a
  month comes out of it.
- The QR scanner reads the box's code at distances and angles where it did not. Until now a
  dense code (the enrolment code is 57 to 61 modules a side) read only in a narrow band: close
  enough to resolve, not so close that it filled the frame, and held square. Four causes, all in
  the app's own reader, found by drawing a real code into camera-sized frames and counting what
  read:
  - The scan for the three corner squares tried only every third dark run on a line as a
    square's beginning. On a code shown on a dark page, the corner beside the page was never
    found by any line through it. "Two corners of three" was the scanner's commonest near-miss.
  - A dense code's own data contains dozens of places that look like a corner along one row
    and one column, and three of those often made a neater right angle than the real corners.
    A real corner reads as one along its diagonals too, so those are tried first.
  - A turned code's module size was measured on a slant, which put its size out by a version
    or more. A code at forty-five degrees never read.
  - A speck of data could be taken for the small square near the fourth corner, and the grid
    was then sampled askew. The grid is also sampled from the three corners alone, and the
    decoder judges.

  Out of fourteen frames each, before and after: at 8 pixels a module 3 and 14; at 10 pixels 0
  and 13; at 3 pixels turned twenty degrees 1 and 12; at 6 pixels turned forty-five degrees 0
  and 13; at 3 pixels, slightly blurred, 2 and 14. Pointing at nothing costs under a third of
  the work it did, which is what warmed the phone during a long scan.
- The scanner's hints go when the code does. "Move closer" used to stay on the screen after the
  code had left the frame, and was still there the next time the scanner opened. It now also
  shows for the case that needs it most, a code so far away that only two corners resolve, and
  "move back" starts where a code of that size stops fitting the frame, not before.
- "Camera busy, tap to retry" retries on a tap (it did nothing). If the camera was refused, the
  scanner offers to ask again, and once Android has stopped asking, opens the app's page in
  the phone's settings; the scanner starts by itself when you come back.
- The map moves more smoothly. Dragging or pinching used to rebuild the whole screen around
  the map on every frame, restart three background tasks and work out the wanted map tiles
  again, all to redraw one layer. The camera is now watched from outside the screen's layout:
  a moving map redraws and nothing else happens, and the tiles under the view are worked out
  again only when its edge crosses into a new tile.
- The map is quieter, so your trail is easier to follow, and the trail itself is as it was. Each
  kind of road now comes in at its own zoom, fading in faint and thin, then drawn in full as you
  get closer. On a phone in London, for example: motorways and trunk roads from the country view,
  A roads from about 60 km across, B roads from 17 km, smaller through roads from 9 km, the
  streets from about 4 km, paths and service roads closer still. Every street used to come in at
  about 17 km, and every street's name at 6 km. A road is named once it is drawn in full: the
  main roads from 6 km, the streets from about 1 km. The roads are greys now; the motorways were
  amber, the trail's own colour, and a day on a motorway disappeared into it. Zoomed right in,
  everything is drawn as before.
- GALLERY lists every photo of a burst. The list is read a page at a time, each page starting
  after the last photo of the one before, and "after" was by the time alone: when a page ended
  inside several photos taken in the same second, the rest of that second was never listed.
  The same for a day's photos on its page. The box now pages by time and hash.
- Three lists could stop the app on a repeated row: a strip of photos holding the same photo
  twice, two coins whose symbol and rank run together to the same text, and a gallery page
  from an older box handing a photo back. Each row's key is unique now.

In the repository and not in the release:

- `app/ios`, an iOS app being built to the Android app's contract (David, 0ex-d, with this
  work): it enrols from the box's QR, opens the box with the PIN over a channel pinned to the
  box's fingerprint, and replaces the QR's key with one made in the iPhone's Secure Enclave as
  Android does. Signed in, it has Android's drawer, HOME (the prices, the brief, the stories,
  FOR YOU, the calendar, the weather for where the phone is), the chat with its saved
  conversations and the short, medium, long switch, Box Status, ABOUT and the GLOSSARY. On a
  pull to refresh it fetches the box's public feeds as the Android phone does. A box that
  locks, or a session that ends, brings the PIN back. It forgets the box on request (the
  key, the session, Home's cache and the chat last opened) and when the app is reinstalled.
  The other screens say they are not built yet; the chat has no web search, attachments or
  voice. It has not been tried against a box, and a release carries no iOS app.
- The engineering journal (`server/tools/DEV_UPDATE.md`) keeps the current month whole and
  each month before it as a line a day. The entries as they were written are in its history,
  and the tag v0.0.8 holds all of them to 13 November.

## Known gaps at 0.0.9

- When the chat's microphone decides you have finished was set by numbers (how loud a voice is
  against a room, 1.8 seconds of quiet), not yet by ear on a phone. It errs toward stopping
  late; cut short, a second tap adds to what is in the field.
- The box's side of a question asked aloud (first in the queue, the language heard again, a
  silent file refused) was tested against a stand-in for whisper, not whisper.
- Everything under [0.0.8's known gaps](0.0.8.md#known-gaps-at-008) still stands; nothing on
  that list is closed yet.
- The scanner still does not read a code turned forty-five degrees at about four pixels a
  module, or one seen at a steep slant (the far side a quarter narrower than the near), or one
  blurred across two pixels at that size. Square-on or straighter, closer or steadier, it does.
- The scanner's changes were measured on drawn frames, not on a phone's camera; the map's on
  how often the screen is rebuilt, not on a frame counter. Both want a look on a real device.
- The map's new road zooms and greys were set by the numbers (the width of a screen at each
  zoom, the colours against the land's), not yet looked at on a phone.
- ghost.shadowd's cold read is by the box's one model until a second is put on the box. The
  box runs a second model when it finds one and no second model ships yet: which one, and its
  place on the mirror, are still to be decided. Until then it reads with its own and says so
  on every record. Taking you out of the question is still the larger part.
- ghost.shadowd's detectors that ask the model (the cold read, the conduct audit) were tested
  against a model that says what the test tells it to. What a real model makes of the
  questions is what `ghost-cli ghost.shadowd try` shows on a box: nine made-up cases through
  the real model, with what was wanted beside what came back. It has not been run on one yet,
  so how often the real model is wrong is not known. The detectors that read words read English.
- SHADOW is in the Android app and not yet in the iOS one, where what it names arrives as a
  notification that opens nothing of its own.
- The three detectors that read what you wrote about other people read words, in English, and
  cannot tell a partner from an employer: "I'm not allowed to" is both. They say so, and that
  is why three sentences are shown to you and nothing more is concluded.
- Sixteen of the catalogue's thirty patterns have no detector: the ones that need your
  conversations with other people (the box holds none; for the first three it reads your own
  account of them instead), and the ones that need a classifier for content made to persuade
  (nobody has one that works).
- A trip is made from photos. Two days away with no photo taken are not a trip, though the
  trail knows where the nights were spent; the trail is not asked yet. And a night is told from
  the photos' times: arrive after dark and leave at dawn with fewer than eight hours between
  the first photo and the last, and it is a day out.
- The new rules for outings and trips were tried on made-up weekends, not yet on a real
  archive.
